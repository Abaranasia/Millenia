# FDN vs. Dattorro Tank for Millenia's Shimmer Reverb — Research Notes

**Purpose**: this project's shimmer reverb pitch-shifts its own feedback signal inside a two-branch Dattorro figure-eight tank. That choice has produced a specific, recurring class of problem across Phases 3, 8, and 9 (see `docs/shimmer-reverb-implementation-plan.md`): summing the tank's own direct recirculation with a copy that detours through the pitch shifter's internal delay line causes destructive comb-filtering, worst at Freeze's near-unity decay; and the very weight-blend that causes that interference is *also* what keeps the tank's two-branch cross-feed from resonating past unity gain at that same near-unity decay. Before starting Phase 10's buffer-capture-and-loop redesign, the owner asked whether a Feedback Delay Network (FDN) core would sidestep this tension more naturally. This document answers that, grounded in real literature and open-source prior art, and is honest about where the evidence runs out.

**Scope note on verification**: every citation below was checked via search and, where possible, by fetching the actual paper/page. Where a claim could not be verified this way, that is stated explicitly rather than presented as fact.

---

## 1. FDN fundamentals

A **Feedback Delay Network** is a recursive filter built from `N` delay lines (`N` typically 4–16, not 2), whose outputs are read, passed through an `N×N` **feedback matrix**, and fed back into the delay lines' inputs, summed with the (multi-tap) input. In matrix form, one time-step of the core recirculation is:

```
s[n+1] = A · z(s[n]) + b·x[n]
y[n]   = c^T · z(s[n]) + d·x[n]
```

where `z(·)` applies each line's own delay (and, in practice, a per-line damping filter), and `A` is the feedback matrix.

**Structural difference from Dattorro/Schroeder**: a Dattorro plate is, structurally, a *2-line* recirculating network (branch A and branch B) whose "mixing matrix" is a fixed, hard-wired 2×2 cross-feed, with the diffusion work done separately by four *cascaded* (not recirculating) Schroeder allpass stages in each branch and in the input diffuser. This framing — that Dattorro is a small, allpass-augmented, fixed-topology special case of the general FDN idea, not a wholly different structure — matches a widely-repeated characterization on the KVR Audio DSP forum's "Dattorro reverb improvements" thread, which describes Dattorro as "essentially a 2-wide FDN where the delays have been cascaded with a bunch of all-pass filters" (verified via search; forum post, not a peer-reviewed source, but consistent with Dattorro's own 1997 paper structure). A "real" FDN generalizes this in three ways an N=2 fixed-cross-feed tank cannot:

- **N is a free design parameter** (8 and 16 are common), not fixed at 2 by the topology itself.
- **The feedback matrix is a genuine design object**, not a hard-wired cross-feed. For the network to be *lossless* (energy-preserving, hence provably stable before damping is applied), the matrix must be **unitary** (orthogonal, for real-valued coefficients) — Signalsmith Audio's own writeup states this plainly: "An orthogonal matrix is a matrix where the total energy of the output always equals the total energy of the input," and that this energy-preservation property is what prevents the recirculation from ever blowing up before damping is deliberately introduced. Common concrete choices: **Householder** matrices (Signalsmith recommends this specifically because it gives only "moderate mixing without excessive inter-channel coupling" — too much mixing "moves the eigentones of your reverb closer together," which causes coloration in long decays), **Hadamard** matrices (used in the one verified academic shimmer-FDN paper below, at order 16 — see §2), and **circulant** matrices (a standard alternative in the FDN literature, not directly verified in a source fetched for this document).
- **Decay and coloration are decoupled by design**: RT60 (per-line, ideally per-frequency-band) is set by a small damping/shelving filter placed *per delay line*, independent of the matrix — Jot & Chaigne's foundational 1991 paper ("Digital Delay Networks for Designing Artificial Reverberators," AES Convention 90 — verified via search/Semantic Scholar) proposed exactly this: controlling decay via per-line attenuation while extending the unitary-matrix framework "to any recursive delay network having a unitary feedback matrix." A later refinement, Dahl & Jot's "Absorbent All-Pass Filters" (DAFX 2000, verified PDF found at CCRMA), attaches an absorption/low-pass filter to each delay unit so different frequency bands can decay at different target T60s — this is the standard mechanism for the frequency-dependent decay real halls exhibit, and it's a first-class, per-line, per-band control knob in an FDN in a way Dattorro's tank (one shared one-pole leaky-integrator `dampingCoefficient` for the whole recirculation, as `DattorroTank.h` implements) is not.

**Stability in a real system** additionally requires each per-line damping filter to have gain strictly `< 1` at every frequency (the matrix alone only guarantees losslessness, i.e. gain exactly `= 1`); Schlecht & Habets' "Scattering in Feedback Delay Networks" (IEEE/ACM TASLP 2019/2020, arXiv:1912.08888, verified via search) generalizes the matrix concept further to "lossless filter feedback matrices" and introduces a "velvet feedback matrix" specifically to raise echo density (a known FDN weakness at small `N`) at low computational cost — again, only under the same losslessness/unitarity discipline.

## 2. FDN-based shimmer reverbs in the wild

**Verified academic prior art specifically combining FDN + pitch shift ("shimmer")**: Jingjie Zhang, *"'Shimmer' Audio Effect: A Harmonic Reverberator,"* CCRMA MUSIC 421A final project report, Spring 2018 (PDF fetched and read in full for this document). This is the one directly on-topic paper found. Its actual design, however, is structurally **different from what this project needs answered** — see §3 for why this matters:

- It uses **two separate order-16 FDNs** (a "branch" FDN and a "master" FDN), each with an order-16 **Hadamard** feedback matrix.
- The pitch shifter (phase-vocoder-based, ±1 and ±2 octaves) sits **outside and in parallel** with the branch FDN, not recirculating inside a shared loop: signal flow is `input → [1-octave shifter, 2-octave shifter] → branch reverb → 275ms delay → mix with dry → master reverb`. The pitch-shifted content passes through the branch FDN's reverb tail *once*, then is summed with dry and fed through a second, separate master FDN — it is never fed back into a shifter for a second, cascading shift.
- The paper's own future-work section explicitly flags this as unexplored territory: *"it is also interesting to experiment with the location of this module in this system and see its impact on the whole effect"* — i.e. the author never tried putting the shifter inside a true recirculating FDN loop either, so this paper cannot be read as evidence that doing so is safe or well-behaved in an FDN. This is a real gap in the literature, not an oversight in this research: no verified source found here actually built and analyzed a pitch shifter *recirculating inside* an FDN's own feedback matrix, which is precisely Millenia's architecture and precisely where its problems arose.

**Foundational FDN design/stability literature** (verified via search, not all fully fetched):
- Jean-Marc Jot & Antoine Chaigne, "Digital Delay Networks for Designing Artificial Reverberators," AES Convention 90, 1991 (PDF traceable via ResearchGate/Semantic Scholar) — the paper that popularized the unitary-matrix FDN framework this whole area builds on.
- Sebastian J. Schlecht & Emanuël Habets, "Scattering in Feedback Delay Networks," IEEE/ACM TASLP, 2019/2020, arXiv:1912.08888 — generalized/velvet feedback matrices for echo density.
- Sebastian J. Schlecht, "Allpass Feedback Delay Networks," IEEE Trans. Signal Processing 69, 2021, arXiv:2007.07337 (verified via search) — characterizes which FDNs are allpass (flat magnitude response) for arbitrary delay values, and explicitly extends Schroeder's series-allpass and Gardner's nested-allpass designs (i.e. Dattorro-family designs) into the general FDN "uniallpass" framework. This is relevant background for why Valhalla's design (§ below) can claim an exactly-flat per-channel response.
- Dahl & Jot, "Absorbent All-Pass Filters" (aka time-frequency/frequency-dependent absorption in FDNs), DAFX 2000, CCRMA-hosted PDF (verified, found directly at `ccrma.stanford.edu/~lukedahl/pdfs/`).
- Vesa Välimäki et al., "Fifty Years of Artificial Reverberation," IEEE TASLP 20(5), 2012 — the standard survey citing FDN history (Schroeder → Stautner/Puckette → Jot); cited as reference [1] inside the Zhang shimmer paper above, confirming it's treated as the field's standard survey. Content not independently re-verified beyond its confirmed existence and citation context.

**Open-source implementations found and characterized**:
- **Signalsmith Audio, "Let's Write A Reverb"** (ADC 2021 talk + `github.com/Signalsmith-Audio/reverb-example-code`, already cited in this project's own `shimmer-reverb-concepts.md`) — a modern, from-scratch FDN design writeup. Recommends **≥8 internal channels**, a **Householder** matrix in the feedback loop specifically to avoid over-mixing eigentones, a separate multi-channel **diffuser stage** (Hadamard mixing + channel shuffling/polarity inversion) ahead of the feedback loop for stereo decorrelation, and treats damping as an optional shelving-filter extension rather than a built-in per-band system. Notably, **this writeup does not discuss pitch shifting, in-loop nonlinearity, or comb-filtering risk from time-varying elements at all** — it is a clean-room FDN tutorial, not evidence about the shimmer-specific question.
- **chowdsp_utils** (`github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils`, already cited in this project's own docs) — confirmed to contain both a modular FDN/Dattorro-style reverb template and a separate `PitchShifter` class (ring-buffer, two read pointers, per its Doxygen page). What could **not** be verified by this research is that these two components are wired together anywhere in that repository into an actual shimmer engine with the shifter inside the FDN's recirculation — the project's existing docs' description of it as "a Dattorro-style FDN reverb and a ring-buffer pitch shifter" is accurate as a description of two separate available building blocks, not confirmed as a combined shimmer example.
- **freeverb3** (`nongnu.org/freeverb3`) — includes an FDN-based "zita reverb" port and a separate Dattorro/"Progenitor" reverb port, confirming both topologies are actively maintained as alternatives in real open-source code, but no shimmer/pitch-shift integration found in it.
- Smaller/hobby repos found but not deeply verified: `Danial-k-f/Shimmer-Reverb` (ZitaRev core — an FDN-family design — plus "granular pitch-shift layers," described by its own README as a shimmer feedback loop with multi-layer granular pitch shift; not inspected in source), `shabtronic/FDN-Reverb-Riser`, `Squishy47/OpenVerb` (FDN-based algorithmic reverb, no shimmer claim), `stancsz/fdnr-vst3` (FDN reverb inspired by Valhalla's *non*-shimmer plugins). None of these were read at the source-code level for this document; they are listed as leads, not verified case studies.

**Commercial shimmer reverb — Valhalla Shimmer**: Valhalla's own source is closed, so nothing here is a confirmed implementation detail, but one specific, attributable public design statement was found (KVR Audio forum, "Introducing ValhallaShimmer" thread, its designer Sean Costello posting under his own name — verified via search, content not independently re-fetched from the primary thread page beyond the search-engine summary, so treat this as a high-confidence secondary paraphrase rather than a directly-quoted primary source): the reverb algorithm inside Shimmer was **specifically designed to tolerate pitch-shifted feedback**, built so that "each channel is essentially allpass with 0dB frequency response for all frequencies," which is *why* it can run at high feedback with pitch-shifted content recirculating without turning metallic. This is a meaningful data point: it implies that **making a reverb tank behave well with an in-loop pitch shifter was treated by a leading commercial designer as its own deliberate design problem to solve** — not something that came for free from any particular topology choice (Costello's own reverbs, going back to the Lexicon-derived tradition, are generally understood to be allpass/Schroeder-family designs — a close cousin of Dattorro, not a large-N unitary-matrix FDN — though this project could not verify Shimmer's exact internal topology from public sources, so this should be read as suggestive, not conclusive, about which topology family is "correct").

**Related, older, and instructive precedent — frequency-shifter-in-feedback-loop for infinite sustain**: search surfaced (but did not turn up a directly fetchable primary source for) the general audio-engineering technique of putting a *frequency shifter* (as distinct from a pitch shifter) inside a reverb or delay feedback loop specifically to get artifact-reduced infinite sustain, associated with Lexicon-era reverb design history (David Griesinger) and separately documented in acoustic-feedback-suppression literature (Scheuing & Yang, "Frequency Shifting for Acoustic Feedback Reduction," verified PDF found, though that paper is about live-sound feedback suppression, not shimmer reverbs, and recommends shifts under ~12 Hz — not directly transferable to a musical ±24-semitone shimmer range). This is flagged as background context, not as direct evidence for or against FDN: the same "something in the loop needs to not be a perfect echo of itself" problem this project hit has multiple independent lineages in the literature, and topology alone has never been the described fix in any of them — filtering/shifting *strategy* has been.

## 3. Does FDN structurally avoid this project's specific problem?

**Short answer: partially, and only if the shifter is integrated deliberately — not automatically, and not just by having more delay lines.**

Reasoning from the math, cross-checked against the literature above:

1. **The core failure mode is topology-independent.** Millenia's Freeze-decay bug (Phase 9, `DattorroTank.h`'s `frozenMaxShimmerBlendWeight` comment) is destructive interference between two correlated copies of the same recirculating signal arriving at the same summing junction via two different delay lengths (the tank's own ~0ms-relative direct path vs. a copy detoured through `PitchShifter`'s own ~80ms internal delay line). That is comb-filter math (`H(z) = 1 + z^-tank_path + g·z^-(tank_path+80ms)`, roughly), and it doesn't care whether the summing junction belongs to a 2-line Dattorro cross-feed or an 8-line FDN's per-line input adder. **Any topology that sums a pitch-shifted copy of a recirculating signal back with an unshifted copy of the same signal at one point will have some comb-filter structure at that point.** An FDN does not have a structural property that makes this specific interference vanish.

2. **What an FDN's matrix genuinely changes is *distribution*, not elimination.** Schlecht & Habets' whole "scattering"/velvet-matrix research direction (§2) exists precisely because small-`N` FDNs have too little cross-coupling and too sparse an echo density — the matrix's job is to spread a single input impulse's energy across many delay lines and many later taps instead of a few dominant ones. Applied to this project's problem: if the shifted-feedback signal were injected into its *own dedicated delay line(s)* within an N-line FDN (rather than added directly into the same per-line input node that also carries the tank's own direct feedback, which is structurally what `DattorroTank::processSample()`'s `inputToA = diffused + decayGain*(plainWeight*feedbackFromB + shimmerWeight*externalFeedback)` does today), the matrix would mix that line's energy into *every* other line on the next step rather than only interfering with one specific counterpart path. That plausibly turns one deep, audible comb notch (or few) into many shallower, less correlated ones spread across the spectrum — closer to Schlecht's own "decorrelation via filter feedback matrices" framing (verified via the Aalto "Decorrelation in Feedback Delay Networks" abstract found in search: "small FDNs with a few feedback channels tend to have a high inter-channel correlation, and the use of a filter feedback matrix significantly improves the decorrelation"). This is a *reasoned mechanism*, consistent with the literature's general claims about decorrelation — it is **not** a claim backed by a paper that built and measured exactly this shimmer-in-FDN case, because no such source was found (see §2's gap).

3. **The theoretical stability guarantee an FDN offers doesn't survive contact with a real pitch shifter anyway.** The entire "unitary matrix ⇒ lossless ⇒ provably bounded before damping" argument (Jot 1991, Signalsmith's write-up) is a **linear, time-invariant** argument: it holds for a fixed matrix and fixed (or slowly-varying) per-line filters. `PitchShifter` is neither — it is a WSOLA-style, per-grain, time-varying, imperfectly-energy-preserving process (this project's own dev history is full of exactly this: the Phase 3 "detuned pitch" phase-incoherence bug, the Phase 7 metallic-sideband bug, both real, measured departures from clean unity-gain, phase-coherent recirculation). The moment a real pitch shifter sits inside *any* feedback network's loop, you are outside the regime where the matrix's unitarity gives you a stability proof "for free," regardless of whether `N=2` or `N=16`. Practical stability then comes back down to the same kind of thing this project already had to do empirically: cap how much of the loop's gain budget the shifted path can claim (Millenia's `shimmerWeight`/`plainWeight` crossfade, capped at 0.85, with a further Freeze-specific cap swept down to 0.01), add a safety limiter, and tune by ear/measurement. An FDN would still need an equivalent of that; it would not eliminate the need for it.

4. **The corroborating practitioner evidence points the same direction.** The one piece of direct anecdotal evidence found (the Csound mailing-list "Shimmer reverb and controlled feedback loop" thread, §2) has a contributor recommending building from an FDN-like parallel-comb structure specifically so the pitch shifter can be placed "within the reverb's internal feedback loop" without "the same problems with overloading" — a real practitioner intuition that N-line structures tolerate this better — but the thread's own resolution needed a *separate*, explicitly gain-limited "frozen reverb" stage layered on top, and still reported "a minor clicking artifact when disabling the freeze effect." That is: even the FDN-leaning solution in the one real precedent found still needed its own bespoke gain-staging and freeze-specific handling — not dramatically less than what Phase 8/9 already built for Dattorro.

5. **The Zhang CCRMA paper (the one verified academic FDN-shimmer build) sidesteps the exact question by construction.** It never recirculates the shifted signal through the same FDN more than once — no cascading, no shifted-signal-summed-with-direct-feedback junction at all. That is itself informative: the one academic author who actually built an FDN shimmer chose an architecture that structurally avoids the in-loop-cascading-pitch-shift problem, rather than one that solves it. That is weak evidence that even in FDN-land, recirculating a real pitch shifter through the network's own feedback path is considered nontrivial enough to route around rather than confront.

**Conclusion for this section**: an FDN's larger `N` and structured matrix would very plausibly *soften* the comb-filtering artifact (spread it across more, shallower notches instead of concentrating it) and would give independent per-line/per-band damping as a genuinely useful extra tuning axis this project's single shared `dampingCoefficient` doesn't have — both real, structural advantages. But it does **not** remove the fundamental tension (a non-ideal, time-varying element recirculating in a lossless-by-design network breaks the stability guarantee that network exists to provide), and no verified source in this research actually demonstrates an FDN handling this specific in-loop-cascading-shimmer case cleanly. Anyone claiming FDN "just solves" this problem is not backed by anything found here.

> **Note (follow-up research, §9):** a specific claim that Valhalla Shimmer combines an FDN with an in-loop *granular* pitch shifter was investigated directly. The granular part is confirmed by Sean Costello's own words; the FDN-topology part is plausible but unconfirmed for Shimmer specifically. More importantly, that investigation surfaced a real, separable, non-topology-dependent technique — **randomizing grain timing to decorrelate recirculating artifacts** — that this section's own reasoning did not consider and that could plausibly help Millenia's actual measured problems without any topology change. See §9 for the full analysis; it refines this section's conclusion rather than reversing it.

## 4. Comparison table

| Dimension | Dattorro tank (current) | FDN (N≈8–16, unitary matrix) |
|---|---|---|
| Diffusion quality / echo density buildup | Fast, proven: 4-stage input diffuser + per-branch allpasses give dense diffusion in a handful of stages; well-documented, exact published coefficients exist (Dattorro 1997) | Naturally sparser at small `N` (echo density grows roughly with `N` and matrix mixing quality); needs a separate diffuser stage (as Signalsmith's design does) or a scattering/velvet matrix (Schlecht & Habets) to reach comparable density — an extra design layer Dattorro gets "for free" from its allpass cascades |
| Per-band decay/damping tunability | One shared one-pole leaky-integrator coefficient for the whole recirculation (`DattorroTank::dampingCoefficient`) — coarse, single knob | Native per-line (and, with absorbent-allpass/shelving filters per Dahl & Jot, per-frequency-band) damping — this is a structural FDN strength with no Dattorro equivalent short of adding filters per branch by hand |
| Ease of stereo decorrelation | Needs a bolted-on trick (Millenia uses quadrature-offset pitch-shift voices reading the same tank, per Phase 4) since there is only one mono tank | More natural: extra channels are already there: N≥4 lines let you tap different channels per ear, or use a dedicated diffuser/shuffle stage (Signalsmith's Hadamard+polarity-flip approach) without inventing a new decorrelation mechanism |
| Computational cost | Low: ~10 delay lines + allpasses total, one damping filter, well inside real-time budget already proven in this project | Higher: 8–16 delay lines each with fractional interpolation, one `N×N` matrix-vector multiply per sample (or per block), plus per-line damping filters — a real but usually acceptable CPU increase; Signalsmith explicitly designs for real-time use at this scale, so "higher" does not mean "impractical" |
| Implementation complexity | Already built, tuned, and unit-tested across 9 phases in this project; delay lengths and coefficients are known-good and verified against a reference port | A rewrite from near-zero for this project: matrix choice, per-line delay-length tuning (mutually-prime-ish, same discipline as Dattorro but across more lines), new per-line damping design, new stereo tap-out design — none of it reusable from the current tank |
| Suitability for an in-loop pitch shifter specifically | Empirically fought to a workable (if imperfect) state through Phases 3/8/9's long, measured investigation — comb filtering at Freeze remains a "genuine partial fix... not a full resolution" per the plan doc | No verified prior art actually does this cleanly (see §2/§3); theoretically plausible that dedicating specific lines to the shifted path would decorrelate the interference better, but this is reasoned, not demonstrated |
| Known characteristic artifacts | Metallic ringing if delay lengths are too regular (mitigated by Dattorro's published mutually-prime-ish values); at near-unity decay, structural comb filtering between direct and shifter-delayed feedback (this project's own measured Freeze finding) | Small-`N` FDNs are documented to suffer audible "ringing"/modal coloration from too-sparse eigenvalue spacing unless a diffuser or scattering matrix is added (Schlecht & Habets' whole research motivation); Householder-specific over-mixing risk if too many channels are coupled per Signalsmith's own caution |

## 5. Honest pros/cons of switching to an FDN core

**Pros, specific to this project's actual pain points**:
- Per-line/per-band damping would give an independently tunable "how bright/dark does the shimmer path sound as it cascades" knob that the current single `dampingCoefficient` cannot express — directly relevant to the Phase 8 backlog item ("a shimmer-specific ADSR/envelope... independent of the plain tail's own decay") already flagged as wanted but not designed.
- More lines plausibly spread the Freeze-decay comb interference into more, shallower notches instead of concentrating it in one measured ~-6.7dB-over-15s decay hole — a real, literature-consistent (if not directly proven for this exact case) mechanism.
- Native multi-channel structure removes the need for the quadrature-pitch-voice decorrelation hack, simplifying `PitchShifter`'s stereo story.

**Cons, specific to this project's actual situation**:
- **It would not, by itself, remove the need for the empirical gain-budget engineering this project already did.** Section 3's conclusion is the central fact here: the stability guarantee FDNs are famous for is a linear/time-invariant one that a real pitch shifter inside the loop breaks regardless of `N`. Phases 8–9's `shimmerWeight`/`plainWeight` crossfade-and-cap discipline, the safety limiter, the DC blocker, and the Freeze-specific weight sweep would all likely need direct analogues in an FDN version — this is new tuning work, not work an FDN topology eliminates.
- **This project has 9 phases of hard-won, measured, working knowledge invested in the Dattorro tank** — exact delay lengths cross-checked against a reference port, a damping coefficient corrected from a 600x bug, an output-tap formula fixed from a "sounds like a delay" bug, and (per Phase 9) a `frozenMaxShimmerBlendWeight` value found only by a careful, non-monotonic sweep that a short test alone would have gotten wrong (`0.007f` "looked fine on the committed 3-minute test but failed a 10-minute extended run"). An FDN rewrite starts essentially all of that over: new delay-length tuning, new damping design, a from-scratch stability sweep for its own version of the Freeze case.
- No verified open-source or academic implementation of exactly "pitch shifter cascading inside an FDN's own recirculation" exists to port from or validate against (§2's gap) — this would be closer to novel design work than an established pattern to follow, unlike Dattorro's tank which had a directly-verified reference port to cross-check against from day one of this project.
- The musical goal (an ethereal, blended, ascending wash) is already reported by the user as "sounds great now" at the current Freeze/shimmer tuning (Phase 9's closing note) — the remaining known gaps are Phase 10's scope (true loop-capture) and a few explicitly-deferred ear-tuning items (Width's character, downward-shift audibility), none of which an FDN rewrite is obviously better positioned to fix than continuing on the current tank.

## 6. Direct answer: would FDN have solved this more easily, or achieved the goal more accurately?

**Would it have solved the freeze-decay/comb-filtering/stability tension more easily than the current path? Probably not "more easily" — and the evidence does not strongly support that it would have avoided the tension at all.** The comb-filtering mechanism this project measured and fixed (comparing the tank's direct feedback against a copy delayed by the shifter's own ~80ms buffer) is a property of summing two correlated, differently-delayed copies of the same signal — that is true of any recirculating network, `N=2` or `N=16`. The FDN literature's stability guarantees are built on an idealized lossless, time-invariant matrix; a real pitch shifter recirculating in the loop is neither, so those guarantees don't transfer cleanly regardless of topology. No source found in this research actually built and measured this exact case (a pitch shifter cascading inside an FDN's own feedback matrix) to say otherwise — the one directly relevant academic paper (Zhang, CCRMA 2018) explicitly avoided this exact configuration by using a single-pass, non-cascading shift through a separate branch reverb instead. Given that gap, claiming FDN would have made this "easier" would be asserting something the evidence doesn't back.

**Would it achieve the musical goal (an ethereal, sustained, harmonically-shifted wash) more accurately?** Plausibly *somewhat* better on two specific, literature-grounded axes — independent per-band damping (a real missing control today) and better echo-density/decorrelation distribution of whatever comb artifacts remain — but this is a difference of degree, not of kind, and the current tank has already reached "sounds great now" per the user's own Phase 9 closing feedback. There is no strong evidence that the musical ceiling of a well-tuned Dattorro-with-shimmer is meaningfully lower than a well-tuned FDN-with-shimmer for this specific genre of effect; commercial shimmer reverbs are built on both allpass/Dattorro-family designs (the evidence around Valhalla Shimmer, §2, points this direction, though unconfirmed) and, per this project's own `shimmer-reverb-concepts.md`, some designers do use FDN-style tanks too. **The honest position: the evidence collected here does not strongly favor either topology as the thing standing between this project and a better-sounding shimmer.** The evidence does support that per-band damping and richer decorrelation are real, missing capabilities in the current tank worth wanting eventually — just not evidence that switching topologies now is the way to fix the specific bugs already fought through.

> **Note (follow-up research, §9):** this section's answer is about *topology* only (Dattorro vs. FDN) and stands unchanged. §9 separately investigates a *technique*-level question — whether Valhalla's granular pitch shifter does something Millenia's own grain-pool shifter doesn't (specifically, randomized grain/splice timing) — and finds a genuine, independent, and potentially higher-leverage lead than the topology question this section addresses. Read §9 before concluding "no significant improvement is available here."

## 7. Rough effort estimate for a fork-based FDN core replacement

**This is a rough, non-committal estimate — not a plan.** Assumes a fork/branch attempt, keeping the existing `PitchShifter`, `DCBlocker`, `SafetyLimiter`, `FreezeLeveler`, and the whole `Parameters`/`PluginEditor`/APVTS layer, and replacing only `DattorroTank`'s internals with an N-line FDN behind the same `process()`/`processSample()`-shaped interface `ShimmerReverbEngine` already expects.

|---|---|---|---|
| FDN core implementation (N delay lines, matrix multiply, per-line damping filters, `prepare`/`reset`) | Medium | 3–5 | Structurally simpler code than Dattorro's allpass cascade math, but new; needs its own diffuser stage (Signalsmith-style) to reach comparable echo density to what Dattorro already has |

| Rewriting/porting the DSP unit test suite (`DattorroTankTests.cpp`'s ~equivalent for the new core, plus every `ShimmerReverbEngineTests.cpp` case that assumes `DattorroTank`'s specific API/behavior) | Medium | 2–4 | Test *shapes* carry over (boundedness, decay-to-silence, freeze-boundedness, bare-tank-equivalence); the specific thresholds and internal API calls do not |

| UI/parameter changes (new/renamed knobs if per-band or per-line controls are exposed) | Small–Medium | 1–2 | Mechanical once the DSP-side parameter set is decided, per this project's existing Phase 6/8 pattern |

Treat this as an order-of-magnitude sanity check, not a committed schedule.

## 8. Recommendation

**Proceed with Phase 10 as planned; do not pause for an FDN core rewrite.**

Reasoning:
- Section 3's central finding is that the specific problem this project fought through (comb filtering between a tank's direct feedback and a shifter-delayed copy, and the resulting stability tension at near-unity decay) is a property of *recirculating a real pitch shifter through any feedback network*, not a defect specific to the two-branch Dattorro topology. No verified source in this research shows an FDN avoiding this class of problem in the exact configuration this project uses (shifter cascading inside the network's own loop) — the one academic paper that combines FDN and shimmer sidesteps that exact configuration entirely.
- Phase 10's own goal — true buffer capture-and-loop for Freeze — is a different, and for the "ever-morphing drone" and "sounds like a repeating loop, not a decaying drone" complaints, a more *directly targeted* fix than a topology change would be: it replaces the near-unity-decay recirculation (the actual source of the measured comb-filtering/oscillation problem) with an exact, captured, crossfaded loop that does not depend on sustained near-lossless recirculation through the tank at all. That sidesteps the Freeze-specific instability class structurally, in a way an FDN swap would not, because Phase 10's mechanism removes the near-unity-feedback regime that causes the comb-filtering to become audible in the first place — regardless of which tank sits underneath it.
- This project has nine phases of measured, working, ear-validated tuning invested in the current tank, including hard-won numeric knowledge (the `frozenMaxShimmerBlendWeight` sweep's non-monotonic behavior and the 3-minute-vs-10-minute test-duration trap) that would need to be effectively re-earned from scratch for a new topology, per §7's estimate, for benefits (per-band damping, marginally better artifact distribution) that are real but incremental, not fixes for the specific bugs already resolved.
- The per-band-damping and richer-decorrelation advantages an FDN genuinely offers (§4/§5) are legitimate and worth keeping in mind as a *future* direction — e.g. if Phase 10's loop-capture redesign still leaves an appetite for a richer, more diffuse, more independently-tunable tail — but they are not blocking or urgent relative to Phase 10's already-scoped, more directly-targeted fix for the actual open complaint.

If the owner wants a lower-risk way to keep the FDN option open without committing to the full rewrite now: the Phase 8 backlog's already-noted "shimmer-specific ADSR/envelope" and "user-controllable shimmer sustain/decay" items would be a reasonable place to *first* prototype a small per-band damping filter added to the existing Dattorro tank (cheap, reversible, no topology change) before deciding whether a full N-line rewrite is still wanted after Phase 10 ships.

**Update from follow-up research (§9): one more low-risk, high-potential-value experiment belongs on this same "try before rewriting" list.** §9 finds that Valhalla's own granular pitch shifter (confirmed by Sean Costello's own words) very likely uses *randomized* grain/splice timing specifically to decorrelate recirculating artifacts, whereas Millenia's `PitchShifter` is entirely deterministic (fixed hop schedule, a correlation-driven but non-random WSOLA alignment search). This is a plausible, independently-actionable lead on the Freeze-decay comb-filtering symptom specifically, testable as a small, reversible change inside the *existing* Dattorro tank — no topology change required, and cheaper to try than either the per-band-damping prototype above or Phase 10 itself. See §9.4/§9.5 for the concrete, scoped proposal; consider trying it before or alongside Phase 10, not instead of it.

### 9.3 Reconciling with §2's "allpass, 0dB response" finding — tension, or two true things?

Not a genuine contradiction, on the evidence gathered — most likely **two compatible descriptions of the same general class of object**. §2 already cites Schlecht's 2021 "Allpass Feedback Delay Networks" paper (arXiv:2007.07337), which formally shows how to construct FDNs that are exactly allpass (flat magnitude response, for arbitrary delay-line lengths) — and explicitly frames this as a generalization of "Schroeder's series allpass and Gardner's nested allpass" designs (i.e., the Dattorro/Schroeder family this project already uses) into the full N-line feedback-matrix FDN formalism. That means "each channel is essentially allpass with 0dB frequency response" (§2's KVR paraphrase of Costello's reverb design, attributed there to secondary discussion rather than his own words) and "built from feedback-delay-network-style configurations" (this section's blog paraphrase) are not competing claims — they can both be true of an **allpass FDN**. §1's own framing (Dattorro as a 2-line, allpass-augmented, degenerate case of the general FDN idea) already anticipated exactly this continuum. The most defensible reading: Costello's reverbs plausibly sit somewhere on that same "allpass-preserving delay network" spectrum as Dattorro, likely with more than 2 lines in at least some plugins, rather than representing a wholesale rejection of the allpass/Dattorro family in favor of an unrelated, orthogonal-matrix-only FDN design.

### 9.4 The genuinely separable, actionable question: technique, not topology

The coordinator's framing correctly flags that "should Millenia use a granular pitch shifter" is not a live question — it already has one. `Source/DSP/PitchShifter.h/.cpp`'s own class comment (re-read for this follow-up) documents a SOLA-style finite-lifetime grain-pool design: fixed 20ms grain length, a fixed hop rate (90% of grain length, i.e. `crossfadeFraction = 0.1`), grains launched on a perfectly periodic schedule (`primarySamplesUntilLaunch`/`quadratureSamplesUntilLaunch` counting down from the same `hopSamples` value every time), and — layered on top, added during the Phase 7 "metallic/robotic" fix — a **deterministic WSOLA phase-alignment search** (`findAlignmentOffset()`) that, at every grain launch, picks the single best-correlating splice offset from a small candidate window and reuses it as the seed for the next search. Nothing in this design introduces randomness anywhere: not grain length, not hop timing, not the alignment search's candidate selection (it is a maximization over a deterministic cross-correlation score, always converging on the single best answer given the same input).

This is a **meaningfully different design goal from what §9.2 indicates Costello's shifter does**, not just a different implementation of the same idea:

- **Millenia's WSOLA search actively suppresses the periodic-modulation artifact** by finding the splice point that makes each grain-to-grain transition as phase-coherent as possible — this is precisely what fixed the Phase 7 "metallic/robotic" bug (it drove the hop-rate spectral sideband from -20dB down to roughly -45 to -60dB across the tested range, per the plan doc). It is a real, verified, working fix for *that* bug.
- **Costello's approach (per §9.2) does the opposite on purpose**: rather than finding the one best-correlating splice each time, it deliberately randomizes grain size/splice timing so that whatever periodic artifact *would* appear at a fixed hop rate gets smeared into a noise-like band instead of concentrated at one frequency (and its harmonics). Mechanistically, this is the same general decorrelation principle the FDN literature already uses at the *matrix* level (Schlecht & Habets' whole "scattering"/velvet-matrix research direction, §1–§2: spread correlated energy so it can't concentrate into an audible artifact) — just applied at the *grain-scheduling* level instead. It is also the same general principle behind well-established modulated-delay techniques (LFO-jittered delay lines in chorus/flanger design, and the frequency-shifting-for-feedback-reduction literature surfaced in §2) for breaking up a static comb notch by making the delay path's effective length wander slightly over time instead of staying perfectly fixed.
- **These two techniques target different symptoms, and that distinction matters for what to try next.** Millenia's WSOLA fix targets artifacts *internal to the shifter's own grain-to-grain splicing* (the Phase 7 bug) — it does not, and by its deterministic nature cannot, address the *separate*, later-diagnosed Freeze-decay bug: comb filtering between the tank's own direct feedback and a copy detoured through the shifter's fixed ~80ms `baseDelaySamples` (see `DattorroTank.h`'s `frozenMaxShimmerBlendWeight` comment, and §3's discussion). That specific bug is a static-delay-offset interference problem between *two different signal paths* (tank-direct vs. tank-via-shifter), not a grain-splice artifact within the shifter alone — so grain-window randomization by itself (Costello's specific stated technique) would plausibly help the *character* of the shifted content generically, but would not obviously touch the *static ~80ms comb null* that Freeze's near-unity decay makes audible, because that null's location is set by the shifter's *average* base delay, which grain-window randomization doesn't change.
- **What would plausibly help the Freeze-specific bug** is a related but distinct technique: **jittering/dithering the shifter's mean/base delay itself** by a small, slowly-varying (or per-grain-randomized) amount, so the ~80ms offset between the direct and shifted feedback paths isn't a single fixed value but wanders slightly over time — converting a static, narrow, fully-destructive notch into a slowly-moving one, which is far less perceptible (this is the standard mechanism modulated all-pass/comb delays use to hide comb coloration in chorus and some reverb diffuser designs, and is conceptually the same principle Griesinger-era frequency-shifting-in-feedback-loops and Schlecht's scattering-matrix work both lean on — surfaced in §1/§2's search, not independently re-verified as applied to this exact case). This is a **hypothesis grounded in general, well-established DSP principle, not a technique independently confirmed to be what Costello does** — it is offered as the more directly relevant experiment for Millenia's *specific measured* Freeze symptom, distinct from (and complementary to) grain-window randomization for the *general* shimmer-character question.

### 9.5 Direct answer

**On the topology question (FDN + granular, as one composite claim): the claim does not hold up as stated.** The granular half is confirmed (Costello's own words); the FDN half is plausible as general design philosophy but unconfirmed for Shimmer specifically; and no primary source describes them as one integrated, specifically-verified architecture. §3's and §6's conclusions — that topology alone (FDN vs. Dattorro) does not remove the fundamental in-loop-pitch-shift tension — stand unchanged and are not undermined by anything found in this follow-up.

**On the technique question: yes, this follow-up surfaced something real and separable, worth calling out on its own.** Millenia's `PitchShifter` is entirely deterministic where the best available evidence indicates Costello's shifter is deliberately randomized, and that randomization targets exactly the class of problem (periodic artifacts from a pitch shifter recirculating in a feedback loop) this project has spent three phases fighting. This is **independent of the FDN-vs-Dattorro topology question** — it is a change to `PitchShifter`'s own internal scheduling, testable entirely within the current Dattorro tank, requiring no rewrite of `DattorroTank` or `ShimmerReverbEngine`'s structure at all. Two concrete, small, reversible experiments fall out of this, in increasing order of relevance to the specific bugs already fought:

1. **Add small randomization to grain length and/or hop timing** in `PitchShifter` (e.g. jitter `hopSamples`/`grainLengthSamplesInt` by a few percent per grain, or randomize which of the WSOLA search's near-tied candidate offsets gets picked instead of always taking the single best score) — targets the *general* shimmer-character question (does the cascade sound more "orchestral"/rich vs. more mechanical), independent of Freeze.
2. **Jitter/dither the shifter's mean `baseDelaySamples` by a small, slowly-varying amount** — a more targeted experiment aimed specifically at converting the *measured* Freeze-decay static comb null (the tank-direct vs. ~80ms-shifter-path interference) into a wandering, less audible one, without touching `DattorroTank`'s own already-validated `shimmerWeight`/`plainWeight`/`frozenMaxShimmerBlendWeight` stability work.

Neither experiment requires an FDN rewrite, neither is guaranteed to work (no source found tests this exact scenario — these are reasoned hypotheses grounded in real, cited design precedent and general DSP practice, not proven fixes), and both are small enough to try and measure (reusing this project's own existing diagnostic-test discipline — the 15s Freeze-decay-dB diagnostic already exists and would directly show whether experiment 2 helps) before committing to anything as large as Phase 10 or an FDN core rewrite. Given how cheap and reversible these are relative to either of those, they are worth trying first.
